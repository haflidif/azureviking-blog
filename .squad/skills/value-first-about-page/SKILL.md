# Value-First About Page Structure

**Category:** Content Strategy  
**Created:** 2026-04-21  
**Last Updated:** 2026-04-21

## What This Is

A reusable structural pattern for professional blog "about" pages that prioritizes reader value over author chronology. Shifts from CV-style timeline ("I was born... I worked at... I got certified...") to reader-focused positioning ("Here's what this blog offers and why I'm qualified to deliver it").

## When to Use This Pattern

- When writing or restructuring a professional blog's about page
- When the current about page reads like a LinkedIn profile or resume
- When readers need to quickly understand "what will I learn here?" and "why should I trust this person?"
- When the author has substantial credentials but the chronological presentation buries the value proposition

## The Five-Part Structure

### 1. Value-First Hook (1-2 sentences)

**Purpose:** Immediately tell the reader what the blog does for THEM.

**Pattern:** `[Core belief or problem statement]. That's what this blog is about.`

**Examples:**

- "Cloud infrastructure only works when it's secure by design. That's what this blog is about."
- "Data only inspires change when it matters to your audience. That's where this blog comes in."

**Anti-patterns:**

- ❌ "Welcome to my blog! My name is..."
- ❌ "I'm a [job title] with [X] years of experience..."
- ❌ Generic mission statements that could apply to any blog

### 2. Expertise Framing (1 paragraph)

**Purpose:** Position the author's unique perspective using "intersection" language, then preview the topics readers will find.

**Pattern:** `The intersection of [domain A] and [domain B] has been [the foundation of my career / my focus for X years / what drives my work]. [Explain what kinds of problems you help solve]. Expect insights on [topic list]. The focus here is on [what makes your approach different].`

**Examples:**

- "The intersection of infrastructure and security has been the foundation of my career helping organizations build resilient cloud platforms. ... Expect practical insights on Azure Infrastructure, Cloud Security, Infrastructure as Code, and Microsoft 365. The focus here is on real-world solutions, not theoretical best practices that fall apart on contact with production."
- "The intersection of people and information shapes everything. ... Expect insights on choosing the right tools, methods, and solutions for your specific challenges. The focus here is on Microsoft data platform technologies..."

**Key elements:**

- Name the intersection (gives readers a mental map)
- List topics they'll see (sets expectations)
- Differentiate your approach (why this blog, not others)

### 3. Voice/Credibility (1 paragraph)

**Purpose:** Answer "why should I listen to you?" using credentials in service of trust, not timeline.

**Pattern:** `A bit about the voice behind these posts: [Current role], [Key recognition or community milestone]. [Additional credentials that support expertise]...`

**Examples:**

- "I'm a Senior Cloud Solution Architect at Microsoft, working in the Customer Success Unit. I was a Microsoft MVP in both Azure and Security (double MVP recognition in June 2024). I co-founded the Microsoft Security User Group with over 1,000 members, contribute Terraform modules to the community, and speak at conferences when I get the chance."
- "As a Microsoft Certified Trainer, international speaker, and speaker trainer, much of this work happens in the global data community. Microsoft has recognized these contributions with the MVP award in the Data Platform category since 2018."

**What to include:**

- Current role (establishes present authority)
- Key recognition or milestone (MVP, major community contribution, etc.)
- Community proof (user groups, conferences, open source)

**What to CUT:**

- Chronological job history ("In 2018 I joined... then in 2023 I moved to...")
- Certification lists (these are LinkedIn material)
- Educational background (rarely relevant for blog credibility)

### 4. Where to Find Me (1 paragraph)

**Purpose:** Show active community presence beyond the blog. Demonstrates ongoing engagement, not just past credentials.

**Pattern:** `You can find me [at international conferences / hosting the X user group / co-hosting the Y podcast / through my courses on Z].`

**Examples:**

- "You can find me sharing knowledge here on this blog, contributing to open-source projects, or helping organizations solve cloud infrastructure challenges."
- "You can find me at international conferences, hosting the Swedish Power BI and Fabric User Group, co-hosting the Knee-Deep in Tech podcast, or through one of my courses on Pluralsight."

**Key:** This should feel like an invitation, not a resume bullet. "You can find me..." is warmer than "I speak at..." or "I host...".

### 5. Tiny Personal Note (1 sentence)

**Purpose:** Humanize the author without oversharing. This is the "I'm a real person" moment.

**Pattern:** `[Location or origin] + [one personal detail that feels authentic]`

**Examples:**

- "Life outside of Azure happens in Norway. I'm Icelandic by birth, Norwegian by residence, and permanently caffeinated by necessity."
- "Life outside of data happens in Linköping, Sweden. I live with a wife, three cats, and an embarrassing amount of Star Wars memorabilia."

**Guidelines:**

- Keep it light and brief (1 sentence, max 2)
- Choose a detail that reflects personality (hobby, quirk, family structure, cultural identity)
- Avoid heavy topics (political views, health issues, financial struggles)
- Match the author's natural voice (don't force humor if the rest of the bio is formal)

### Bonus: Disclaimer (if needed)

**Position:** After the personal note, not before it.

**Why:** Legal disclaimers are important but shouldn't be the first thing readers see after learning about the author. Put the human connection last, then the legal boilerplate.

## Voice Considerations

When adapting this structure for a specific author, ALWAYS respect their established voice patterns:

- **For Haflidi (AzureViking blog):** Use contractions naturally, keep most sentences short (8-12 words), no AI filler, no dashes between words. The tone is warm and conversational, not formal corporate.
- **For other authors:** Read their existing blog posts, emails, or professional writing. Extract sentence length patterns, recurring phrases, formality level, and humor tolerance. The structure is universal; the voice must be personal.

## What This Pattern Replaces

### ❌ CV-Style Chronological Bio

"My name is [Name], a [current title] at [company]. I began my career in IT at age [X]. In [year], I moved to [place] and joined [Company A]. In [year], I was promoted to [role]. In [year], I joined [Company B]. Throughout my career I have achieved [certification list]."

**Problem:** Reads like a LinkedIn profile. Prioritizes the author's career progression over the reader's needs.

### ❌ Generic Welcome Message

"Welcome to my blog! I'm passionate about [topic] and love sharing my knowledge. In this blog you'll find tips, tricks, and insights about [topic]. I hope you find it useful!"

**Problem:** No expertise positioning, no credibility markers, no personality. Could be anyone's blog.

### ❌ Pure Credential Dump

"Microsoft MVP. Azure Solutions Architect Expert. Security Engineer Associate. M365 Enterprise Administrator Expert. Speaker at [X conferences]. Author of [Y posts]."

**Problem:** Credentials without context. Readers don't know what the blog offers or why these credentials matter.

## Implementation Checklist

When writing or rewriting an about page using this pattern:

- [ ] **Hook:** Does the opening sentence tell readers what the blog offers THEM?
- [ ] **Intersection:** Is the author's unique perspective framed as the intersection of two domains?
- [ ] **Topics:** Are expected topics listed clearly?
- [ ] **Differentiation:** Is there a "focus here is on..." statement explaining what makes this blog different?
- [ ] **Credentials:** Do the credentials answer "why listen to this person?" without becoming a CV?
- [ ] **Community presence:** Is there active, ongoing community work mentioned (not just past achievements)?
- [ ] **Personal note:** Is there a brief humanizing detail that feels authentic to the author?
- [ ] **Voice match:** Does the tone, sentence length, and word choice match the author's established voice?
- [ ] **No chronology:** Have all "In [year] I did..." timeline statements been removed?

## Examples in the Wild

- **Alexander Arvidsson (arcticdba.se/about):** "Data only inspires change when it matters to your audience..." — opens with reader value, uses intersection framing, ends with Star Wars memorabilia.
- **Haflidi Fridthjofsson (azureviking.com/about):** "Cloud infrastructure only works when it's secure by design..." — security-first value prop, infrastructure+security intersection, caffeinated personal note.

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- ❌ **Timeline openings:** "I was born in [place]...", "I started my career in [year]..."
- ❌ **Generic hooks:** "Welcome to my blog!", "Thanks for stopping by!"
- ❌ **Certification lists:** Save these for LinkedIn. Blog readers care about practical knowledge, not acronyms.
- ❌ **Long credentials paragraphs:** One paragraph of credentials is enough. More feels like resume padding.
- ❌ **Oversharing personal details:** "I'm going through a divorce..." or "I struggle with impostor syndrome..." — keep it light.
- ❌ **Missing the intersection:** Don't just list topics. Frame WHY your combination of expertise is unique.
